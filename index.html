01 · PROBLEM

Meetings end. The work they created leaks away.

Small teams make most of their decisions out loud: in planning calls, standups and Slack threads. Turning those decisions into tasks is a manual, unowned chore that usually falls to the project lead after the meeting, when the details are already fading.

~25 min

spent by a lead after each planning meeting rewriting notes into tasks and pinging owners

1 in 3

action items said out loud never become a tracked task with an owner and a date

4 tools

touched to get one decision from a call into a project board: call, doc, chat, tracker

Problem statement. Project leads at small teams need a fast, trustworthy way to turn what was agreed in a conversation into assigned, dated tasks, because today that step is manual, delayed, and the first thing skipped when the week gets busy.

02 · COMPETITOR RESEARCH

Note-takers stop at the summary. Trackers start at the task.

I compared two groups: AI meeting assistants, which capture conversations well, and project tools, which manage work well. Very few connect the two with a human review step in between.

The gap. Meeting tools produce a list of action items, then hand you a document. Project tools hold the tasks, but someone still has to type them in. The step in between, where a person checks who owns what and by when, is where trust is earned or lost, and nobody designs for it.

Patterns worth borrowing. Timestamped transcript links (meeting assistants), inline editable rows (Linear), and the “inbox to triage” model (email clients, GitHub notifications).

08 · USABILITY DECISIONS

Designing for trust in AI output.

The AI will sometimes be wrong. These decisions make mistakes cheap to spot and cheap to fix, which matters more than raising accuracy by a few points.

D1 · HUMAN IN THE LOOP

Nothing becomes a task until a person confirms it.

Being assigned work you never agreed to is the fastest way to lose a team’s trust. Review is required and can’t be turned off in settings.

Trade-off
One extra step versus full automation.
Heuristic
User control and freedom
D2 · SHOW THE SOURCE

Every item carries the quote and timestamp it came from.

Reviewers judge “did we really agree to that?” in seconds. Owners get the same context in their notification.

Trade-off
Taller cards. Quotes are capped at 2 lines.
Heuristic
Recognition rather than recall
D3 · CONFIDENCE IN WORDS

“Clear commitment”, “Check owner”, “Sounds like an idea”.

A percentage invites debate about the number. A plain label tells the reviewer what to do next and where to look.

Trade-off
Less precision, much faster scanning.
Heuristic
Match between system and real world
D4 · SAFE DEFAULTS

Ideas start unchecked. Inferred fields are highlighted.

The default state is the most likely correct one. Only low-confidence items need an action to include, so a hurried reviewer can’t create junk tasks.

Trade-off
A real task might be missed. The summary still lists it.
Heuristic
Error prevention
D5 · EXPLAIN THE DISABLED BUTTON

The footer says exactly what is blocking creation.

“1 item needs an owner or date” sits next to the disabled button, and the card is outlined. No guessing why nothing happens.

Trade-off
More copy in the footer; kept to one line.
Heuristic
Visibility of system status
D6 · UNDO, NOT “ARE YOU SURE?”

Create immediately, then offer Undo.

A confirm dialog after a review step is redundant. Undo keeps the flow fast and still recovers from mistakes, with edits preserved.

Trade-off
Owners may see a notification that’s then withdrawn; notifications send after the undo window.
Heuristic
Help users recover from errors
D7 · ONE COLOR FOR AI

Amber marks anything FlowPilot wrote.

Summaries, drafts, new-task badges and suggestions all share one color and a spark icon, so people always know what to double-check.

Trade-off
Amber is reserved, so warnings use a separate orange-brown with an icon.
Heuristic
Consistency and standards
D8 · ROLE CHANGES ORDER, NOT ACCESS

Onboarding role picks what Home shows first.

An engineer sees their tasks first; a founder sees project health first. Permissions stay separate, under Settings, so picking a role never locks anyone out.

Trade-off
One more onboarding step. Preview shows the payoff immediately.
Heuristic
Flexibility and efficiency of use

11 · MEASURING SUCCESS

What would tell us this works.

North star
25 min→ 3 min

Median time from meeting end to tasks created, per planning meeting.

Completeness
52%→ 90%

Share of spoken action items that end up as tasks with an owner and a date.

Trust guardrail
< 10%

Tasks reassigned or deleted by their owner within 48 hours of creation.

What I learned. The interesting design problem in AI products is the moment between the model’s output and the user’s commitment. Most of this project’s decisions live in that one review screen.

Next steps

  • Run 5–6 moderated tests with project leads on the review flow, then a 2-week pilot with one team.
  • Design the Slack entry point: review and create tasks from a thread without opening FlowPilot.
  • Explore an owner-side “accept or push back” step for tasks assigned from meetings.
  • Accessibility audit: screen-reader order for the review cards and keyboard-only board moves.
